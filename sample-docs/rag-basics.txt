Retrieval augmented generation, or RAG, has three stages.

First, ingestion: documents are loaded, split into chunks, embedded, and stored in a
vector database. Chunk size matters. Too small and the retriever loses context; too
large and the answer gets diluted by irrelevant sentences.

Second, retrieval: the user question is embedded with the same model, and the closest
chunks are fetched, usually five to ten of them.

Third, generation: the chunks are pasted into the prompt as numbered context and the
language model answers while citing them.

Common failure modes are stale indexes, embeddings computed with a different model than
the query, and context windows stuffed with too many low quality chunks.
